# Terminal remote

Run `bin/thermite remote --mock` to explore a fictional house. Node.js and the locked blessed package are required.

Home shows your rooms and opens Remotes, Security, Cameras or Site. Use the arrow keys to choose, Enter to open, and `?` for the current page's keys. `Tab` changes panes, `PgUp`/`PgDn` scroll, `t` changes theme, and `q` quits. From Remotes, `F6` pairs devices.

In Watch, the remote's pane draws the buttons of the device the room shows. The arrows are its D-pad and Enter its OK, as on the page; the page's other keys (`?` lists them) press the buttons they name. For every other button, `n` and `N` move the focus to the next and the previous one, round from the last to the first, and Space presses it; the focus starts on OK. With the focus on the volume slider (`v` shows it), Left and Right step the volume and Home and End take it to its least and most. A remote taller than its pane draws its keys on one row each instead of as three-row outlines; where rows are still hidden the pane's bottom edge says **PgUp/Dn scroll**, the wheel scrolls it too, and a button the focus moves to is scrolled into view.

Signal & audio (`d`, or beside the remote on a wide terminal) groups the room's signal under the devices that report it, a reading a line with its value at the right edge and a device's age said once; what was not reported is one line naming the readings. A click on a group's heading opens or closes it, and `d` again shows all of the signal, then less; the two current groups stay open, and the choice is kept.

What has failed is part of what a room shows for as long as it lasts, read from the session the page reads (`web/remote/session.js` `view()` and `connected()`). While the house is not heard the remote's pane says **Not connected to thermite. Reconnecting…**, the header's light turns to the problem colour, and every room's, source's and speaker's light becomes ◌: what it showed was read before, so it says neither on nor off and does not blink, and Home shows **Unknown** for every room under the same line. A source that was chosen and sent but never read back keeps a **?** on its bubble, with the alert naming it, until the room reads something else or another choice is made. A device openHAB cannot drive is named in the alert with why, in the words the house gives its problem's code.

A light says its state by its glyph as well as its colour, so it reads without colour (`NO_COLOR`, a console of 16 colours) and in a focused row, which is drawn in the focus's own style: ● on, ○ off, ◐ switching (turning to ◑ and back where motion is on), ! a problem, ? not confirmed and ◌ not heard. The installer's lights are the same ones. On, switching and a problem keep the theme's own colours for them; in xterm's 256 colours the themes thermite ships show them as three colours, none a gray and none the hotkeys' accent. Without colour the focus is reverse video.

Rooms through the day (`h` on Home or Remotes, or the link below Home's rooms) draws the House board's timeline from Grafana: one row per room, its confirmed source over the last 24 hours, not the one requested. A source is tan, with its name where the run is wide enough; Off is slate; Unknown, grey, is a room whose devices gave no readback; a dot is no data. Up and Down choose a room to list its latest runs. It reads the gateway's room history when it opens and every minute while open, and operates nothing. `--mock` draws a fictional day; explicit native testing mode has no gateway to read it from.

Remotes and Home list the rooms of one mode: Watch, the rooms with sources and their remotes, or Listen, the rooms with speakers (a routed room's, then the rooms of speakers alone), each with what it plays. A house with both shows **Watch │ ♪ Listen** in the remote's top edge and beside Home's rooms; click one, or press `y` for the other. The choice is kept for the next start, as the web remote keeps its own; ↓, `[` and `]` walk the mode's rooms, and a room of one mode alone chosen elsewhere brings its mode.

Listen draws the room's speakers as Music Assistant plays them, without covers, else as each speaker reports itself: the speakers' bubbles where there are several (`1`–`9`, or Left and Right outside the remote, choose one), what the chosen one plays and where it is in the track, its transport, the **Group**, **Input** and **Library** chips, and its volume as Watch draws it, with Mute and, in a group, the group's volume. In the remote, Up and Down choose a control, Enter presses it, and Left and Right step a volume or move 10 s through the track; a volume key held repeats. `P` (or Space off a control) plays and pauses, `+` and `-` step the volume, `M` mutes, `<` and `>` change the track, `S` shuffles, `R` (Shift) repeats, `G` opens the group sheet (tick speakers to join, remove one or end the group, set the group's volume; it says why a speaker could not join, and which Music Assistant has not confirmed), and `r` (the rooms sheet, as in Watch) or `[` and `]` choose another room. `/` opens the library at its search field, where what you type, `q` included, is the search: Enter searches at once and `Esc` clears it. The library sheet has Search (with its kinds and sources), Browse and Queue, and an administrator's TIDAL sign-in code. Enter on a folder, album, artist or playlist opens its page over the tab (an artist's top tracks, albums, EPs and singles, compilations and similar artists; an album's numbered tracks; Play, Shuffle and More in its header, and an album's artists to open), and Backspace or `‹ Back` goes back a page, to the entry that opened it; the tab chosen again returns to its own list. Enter on a track offers Play from here (on an album or playlist), Play now, Play next, Add to queue, and Go to its album or artists. Info (`i`) shows the speaker's power, settings and facts; a long choice opens a list. A speaker whose room is locked shows the padlock (⚿) and the lock, as Watch does, and refuses each command with it; a locked room of another speaker in its group refuses what moves the group, not the speaker's own volume or Mute. In Watch, a volume caption that names the room's speaker listens to it when clicked.

From any computer with thermite installed, such as a laptop, run `thermite tui`. It needs no private site: it opens the gateway at `https://thermite:13601`, the name most homes give their Thermite server, and reads the house's rooms and controls from it. Pass `--url` for another name or address, e.g. `thermite tui --url 192.0.2.50` for `https://192.0.2.50:13601`, or `HOST:PORT` for a gateway that `instance/host.yaml` moved off 13601 (`gateway: {port: N}`); with `--config DIR` selecting that private site, its port is the default. The remote is HTTPS only: pointed at a plain-HTTP port, such as a Guest port, it says so. The gateway's certificate comes from the server's private certificate authority, `tls-cert.pem` in its gateway root (`/srv/thermite/access` by default); on the server itself the installation's copy is used, and another computer passes a copy once with `--ca-file`, after which it is remembered for that address. An untrusted certificate stops with the file to copy; verification is never turned off.

For a real house from its private site, run `thermite remote --url https://thermite.example:13601`. Basic controls in unlocked rooms work without a login. A room that is Off selects its Off bubble and draws no remote until a device bubble is chosen for its buttons. In a locked room the bubble for what it shows, Off included, carries a padlock (⚿) in place of its light and every other bubble is greyed out; choosing the padlocked bubble says why it is locked and, for a schedule, until when. The rooms panel and Home show ⚿ in its own column, between a locked room and its source; the rooms panel and sheet show each room's light (on, off or a problem) after its source, at the right edge. Only a lock the terminal has read greys its controls: if a room's lock cannot be read, Remotes says "Room lock status unknown" and still sends commands, which the server refuses in a locked room. To use an account's management permissions, sign in with `thermite access login --url https://thermite.example:13601 --user operator`. Use your own host and account. After changing inventory, run `thermite generate`; the terminal uses your site's generated house model. Credentials stay in an owner-only file.

With authentication enabled, administrators can lock the current room from Remotes with `K`, or with **Lock room** under Info (`i`). The server captures its current reported source or Off and holds it until manually unlocked, including across restarts. To lock on a particular source, right-click its bubble, or Off, or press `o` on it: the menu offers **Lock on** that source, **Unlock room** while manually locked, or **Lock until unlocked** on the bubble a schedule is holding. Locking on another source switches the room first and locks only once the room reports it. The manual lock takes precedence over schedules; releasing it resumes any current schedule. Other users see the lock status without an unlock control.

The terminal reconnects an event stream that has been silent for 15 s (the gateway sends a heartbeat every 5 s), reads the house afresh, and shows anything the gateway does not know as unknown. A command unanswered after 15 s (All off: 30 s) stops waiting and says it may still take effect.

When the terminal gives up waiting on the gateway (a lock read, a room command, an event stream or its first snapshot), or the gateway refuses an event stream, it counts that and reports the counts, never their content, to the gateway after its next answered request, and sends any it has not reported as it quits.

Security (F3) shows authentication status and the browser address for changing it. First setup uses a short-lived code generated on the host by `thermite access setup --descriptor /path/to/access/config.json`, run from the staged release as the thermite service account. Changing authentication signs out every browser and terminal. Pairing, locks and room schedules require authentication. To elevate access, quit, sign in as an administrator with `thermite access login`, and reopen the terminal; an administrator can also make an account an administrator in Users (F8), after which that user must sign in again. Accounts are either administrators or ordinary accounts, which add camera media to what anyone can do in unlocked rooms. Explicit native testing mode cannot change authentication or secure direct runtime access.
